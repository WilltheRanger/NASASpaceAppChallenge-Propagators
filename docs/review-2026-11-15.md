# Hard review of Anchor: 15 November 2026

A blunt review against the five Space Apps judging criteria (Impact, Creativity, Validity, Relevance, Presentation),
plus engineering. Each item says whether it was **fixed** in this pass or is **open**, and roughly what it costs.

## Verdict

The science is the strongest part of the project and the most exposed. The orbit-drift idea is right and current;
NASA's own fire team is still investigating it. The headline number ("Aqua counted 29.6% more than a fixed clock") could
have been a viewing-geometry artifact. The view-angle and fire-power tests below favour a real time-of-day effect,
so show them. And the satellite the method uses as its fixed clock (S-NPP) went dark on
2 November 2026. A NASA judge will know both. Address them head-on and they become strengths; leave them out and they
become the first question.

## Validity (most important)

1. **Mostly resolved (tested 15 Nov): does L measure time of day or sampling?** NASA has evidence for both.
   - **Final:** nine tests in [`analysis/attribution/`](../analysis/attribution/README.md). Clouds and viewing
     geometry are ruled out, and timing accounts for about 90% of the Amazon 2025 excess.
   - Giglio (LANCE UWG, May 2025) lists the drift effects on MODIS: pixel size (about 970 m along-scan by spring 2025),
     wider scan gaps, bowtie overlap, view-angle patterns, and Terra lowered about 6 km in 2022. He also says
     spatial-sampling effects "abruptly increased in magnitude in late 2024".
   - Amazon L jumps from 1.10 (2024) to 1.30 (2025) over exactly that period.
   - Fixed: the README limits now say this and cite the talk.
   - **Done: near-nadir test** ([`analysis/validation/near_nadir_L.md`](../analysis/validation/near_nadir_L.md),
     reproduces published L within 0.0005).
     - Amazon 2025: near-nadir Aqua L = 1.53 (1.34–1.76); off-nadir Aqua L = 0.99 (0.88–1.16). Cerrado 2025
       (1.24 vs 0.97) and Argentina 2025 (1.28 vs 0.98) look the same.
     - Aqua's near-nadir share of detections went from 55.6% (2012–2020) to 66.8% (2025) in the Amazon. Terra's
       went from 51% to 58%. S-NPP's stayed at about 36–37%.
     - Terra's 2025 drop (0.56) is in both near- and off-nadir pixels, which is what a time-of-day effect looks like.
   - **Follow-up done: view angle and fire power**
     ([`analysis/validation/README.md`](../analysis/validation/README.md)).
     - Amazon 2025: Aqua L is 1.39–1.60 in every view bin up to about 33° and 0.89–0.92 beyond.
     - Inside each bin (fixed pixel size), Aqua's median fire power per detection fell 24% at nadir and 15% at
       33–43°. VIIRS's, with an unchanged clock, fell 7–10%.
     - So Aqua's later look sees more, smaller fires, which MODIS detects only near nadir. That points to
       a real time-of-day effect. Terra (now earlier) loses detections at every angle.
     - With NOAA-20 as the reference instead of S-NPP: 1.34 vs 1.30.
   - **What to say:** "Aqua no longer matches the fixed clock, mainly because it looks later: it sees more, smaller
     fires, and the excess sits where MODIS can detect small fires". Show the angle table as evidence. A sampling
     change can't be ruled out, but it isn't needed to explain the pattern.
2. **Fixed in README, open in the app: the fixed clock has stopped.** New S-NPP data stopped on 2 Nov 2026 (the keyless
   S-NPP 7-day feed is empty). MODIS ends January 2027 (Terra) and about September 2027 (Aqua) (CAMS 2026).
   - Without a plan, Anchor can't be run past 2026.
   - **Fix:** estimate a NOAA-20 / S-NPP exchange rate over 2018–2026 and show that NOAA-20 can carry on as the fixed
     clock. `pipeline/download_recent.py --mode api` already pulls the NOAA-20/21 standard archive once someone has a
     FIRMS MAP_KEY. This turns the biggest weakness into a forward-looking claim: "the audit outlives MODIS".
3. **Open: the exchange rate R assumes 2012–2020 behaviour is stable.**
   - R is a per-region, per-month ratio. It is valid only if the diurnal fire cycle and the fire-size mix stay as they
     were. Amazon burning patterns shifted with policy over 2019–2023.
   - The noise floor (one baseline year against the other eight) partly covers this. It does not cover a trend inside
     the baseline. Show R by year within 2012–2020; a flat line is the evidence.
4. **Open: the predictions framing.**
   - 4 of the 10 "predictions" (P1, P3, P8, P9) were written after their results existed, and 3 fail.
   - Reporting it is honest and good. In the pitch, call them "pre-stated checks", or show only the six that were
     pre-registered. A judge who reads "prediction" and then "written after the result" stops trusting the rest.
5. **Fine, keep it:** the INPE cross-check (within 10% in 96.6% of biome-months) is the most convincing number in the
   repo. Put it before the audit result, not after.

## Relevance

- The challenge names three reasons the sensors disagree: resolution, sensitivity, observation schedule. Anchor
  attacks the schedule and folds resolution and sensitivity into one ratio (R).
- Say that explicitly on the first screen, or a judge will think two-thirds of the challenge was skipped. VIIRS sees
  3–4× more fire pixels than MODIS. The Greece pipeline (`data/metadata/data-limitations.md`) shows this ratio is
  stable year to year, which is exactly why one ratio per region-month works. That's a point in your favour; use it.

## Impact

- **INPE's reference-satellite series is the real impact:** a national statistic, used in policy, that is measurably
  biased. Lead with it.
- **"Daylight left after the alert" is the weakest impact claim.** The README itself says INPE uses many satellites,
  including geostationary ones, so a single-satellite alert time isn't what responders act on. Keep it as a
  consequence, not a headline.
- **Open:** name one user and one decision. Example: "an INPE analyst comparing 2025 to 2019 would overstate the rise
  by X points". One number like that beats five features.

## Creativity

- Another 2026 team (FireClock) built a fixed-clock calendar with an Aqua drift check, and the README credits them.
  Your differentiators are the opposite Terra/Aqua drift ("two clocks"), the INPE audit and the agency-fire detection
  limits.
- "Two clocks" failed its simple test (P10). So the clear, *working* differentiators are the INPE audit and the
  detection limits. Make sure the demo shows those first.

## Presentation

- **The limit is 7 slides or a 30-second video.** The app has four pages, an AI assistant, a guided tour, a globe,
  a simulator and a daylight panel. Judges will see perhaps 90 seconds of it.
- **Open: pick one path.** Problem, then INPE check, then L for the Amazon with its interval, then the S-NPP/NOAA-20
  handover. Cut everything else from the demo.
- **Too many coined terms:** "L", "exchange rate", "noise floor", "placebo range", "two clocks", "fixed clock", "Ask
  Anchor". Each one costs a judge attention. Use "drift-corrected count" and "expected range" on the first screen.
